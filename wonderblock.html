WonderBlock: the bonds and the parking

Ogden's biggest recent downtown project, on the old Wonder Bread site at 26th Street and Grant Avenue, from the votes to the paid parking that helps pay for the garages.

Project area: Continental CRAVotes: Jan. 10, 2023Developer: J. Fisher Companies

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHERE
26th Street and Grant Avenue, downtown Ogden
  • Project area: Continental Community Reinvestment Area (created 2019).
  • 2023 votes: RDA: up to $75,000,000 in sales tax and tax increment revenue bonds, 4–3. Municipal Building Authority: up to $85,000,000 in lease revenue bonds, 5–2.
  • Developer: J. Fisher Companies (land transfer and development agreement, 2020; restated Jan. 2023).

In plain words

Ogden's redevelopment agency bought the old Wonder Bread plant in 2016, cleared it, and in 2023 agreed to a joint project with a private developer. The city borrowed for two things: part of the project, repaid from future sales tax and the extra property tax the project creates, and two parking garages, repaid through a lease. To help pay for the garages, the council approved paid parking downtown in May 2026. Property owners keep paying their full tax bill; for the life of the project area, the agreed share of the growth in those taxes goes to the RDA instead of the taxing entities' budgets.

Explain it like I'm 10

  • WonderBlock is a big building project downtown, where the old Wonder Bread factory was.
  • A bond is a big loan that gets paid back over many years.
  • In 2023 the city agreed to two kinds of bonds: up to $75 million for the project and up to $85 million for two parking garages.
  • Tax increment is the growth in property taxes in an area. Owners still pay their full bill. For the set number of years, the agreed share of that growth goes to the project instead of the regular budgets.
  • To help pay for the garages, the council approved paid parking downtown in 2026.

The short version

  • Jan. 10, 2023: the RDA approved up to $75 million in sales tax and tax increment revenue bonds (4–3), and the city's Municipal Building Authority approved up to $85 million in lease revenue bonds (5–2).
  • Jan. 17, 2023: the RDA approved the court parking agreement, operating agreement, and land transfer and development agreement.
  • May 5, 2026: the council approved downtown paid parking, 5–2; KSL reported fees could start about June 15, 2026.
  • KSL reported the garage bonding at about $60.13 million and that parking revenue will help cover the garages' costs and bond payments.
  • The Continental CRA expires in 2047/2058 and showed a $19,061,989 value increase in the RDA's January 2025 annual update.

Paid parking

On May 5, 2026, the council approved downtown paid parking, 5–2. KSL reported street rates of $1.50 to $2 an hour, off-street rates of $1 to $1.50, $10-a-month permits for downtown workers, and fees starting about June 15, 2026 after a trial period. KSL reported the garage bonding at about $60.13 million, and that parking revenue will cover garage operating costs and help cover the bond payments. When the bonds were approved in 2023, a city flyer said the projects "will not use funds from the property taxes of Ogden residents" (KSL, Jan. 11, 2023).

What we don't know yet

  • How each member voted on the 2023 bonds and the 2026 paid parking vote.
  • How much of each bond has been issued so far, and at what rates. (Earlier Hive reporting cited $64.22 million in Series 2023 RDA bonds; that figure wasn't re-checked for this page.)
  • How much paid parking has brought in since it began, and how that compares with the garage payments.
  • Which J. Fisher entity signed each agreement. Earlier Hive research found two Utah companies named "J. Fisher Companies, LLC"; that hasn't been re-checked for this page.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  1. Ogden RDA / Community & Economic Development: How much of the $75 million and $85 million authorizations has been issued, and what are the annual payments?
  2. City Council: Will the city report paid-parking revenue against the garage bond payments each year?
  3. Ogden City Recorder: Do the approved minutes for Jan. 10, 2023 and May 5, 2026 show how each member voted?
  4. Ogden RDA / Community & Economic Development: Which J. Fisher company, by state entity number, signed each WonderBlock agreement?
