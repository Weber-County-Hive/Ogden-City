RECORDS CHECK · PROPERTY VALUE · OC 002

The mayor's home value dropped $151,737 in 2026. Here's what the records show.

A tip came in saying Ogden Mayor Ben Nadolski "had his property tax assessment reduced by $150,000" and that no other Ogden resident got a reduction close to that. The Hive checked it against Weber County's records.

Parcel: 14-108-0013Record: Weber County AssessorTax year: 2026

Posted Nov. 5, 2026 · Updated Nov. 6, 2026

WHAT WAS CHECKED
Weber County parcel 14-108-0013, Ogden, and 24 nearby homes
  • Owner as listed: Benjamin Keith Nadolski & Jaynee P Nadolski Family Trust (primary residence)
  • Records: Weber County parcel search (tax history) and the 2025 and 2026 Notices of Property Valuation
  • Not shown here: the street address. The parcel number is enough to look it up.

In plain words

Every year the county assessor puts a value on every home, and owners get a notice in the summer. The mayor's notice for 2026 showed his home's value down $151,737, or 21%, from 2025. He didn't appeal to get that; it was already on the notice the county mails to everyone. Many of his neighbors' values went down a little too, and some went up, but his went down the most of the homes we checked. The records don't say why.

Explain it like I'm 10

  • Every year the county guesses what each home is worth. That guess is called the market value.
  • Your property tax bill is based on that value.
  • In 2026 the county's value for the mayor's home went down $151,737.
  • He did not appeal. The lower value was already on the notice the county mails to everyone.
  • His drop was the biggest of the 24 nearby homes we checked.
  • The records do not say why.

The short version

  • The tip's dollar figure matches the record: market value went from $721,349 (2025) to $569,612 (2026), a drop of $151,737 (21.0%).
  • It was not an appeal. The lower value is printed on the original 2026 valuation notice. As of Nov. 5, 2026, the county's posted value matched the notice value. The Hive did not check whether an appeal was filed in 2025 or 2026; Board of Equalization decisions can still be pending.
  • The house's listed features didn't change. The notice says the last property review was in 2023.
  • Of 24 nearby homes, most changed between about −12% and +10%. The largest drops were about $72,500. The mayor's drop was the largest in dollars and percent among the nearby parcels with a house (one land-only parcel dropped 22.8%).
  • Whether anyone else in Ogden had a drop that large is not verified; only his neighborhood was checked.

How home values are set in Utah

The county assessor estimates each property's market value every year. State law has counties physically review properties on a regular cycle. The value is mailed to the owner on a Notice of Property Valuation, along with the deadline to appeal. An owner who disagrees can appeal to the county Board of Equalization, which can lower or raise the value. If no one appeals, the notice value is used to figure the tax bill.

Values can change for many reasons: sales of similar homes nearby, a new model for a neighborhood, corrections to the record, or changes to the land or building. The records the Hive read don't say which applied here.

Why there's no map

This page is about a family home. The Hive doesn't map or publish private home addresses, even for public officials. The parcel number lets anyone check the county record.

What we don't know yet

  • Why this home's value fell 21% when nearby homes fell 4–12% or rose, with no property review since 2023.
  • Whether any other Ogden homes dropped this much in 2026. Only one neighborhood was checked.
  • No news coverage of this was found.
  • One neighbor's drop (−$59,575) and the largest rise (+$59,557) have nearly the same digits; both are being re-checked for a typo.
  • Whether an appeal was filed for 2025 or 2026; the Hive did not check.
