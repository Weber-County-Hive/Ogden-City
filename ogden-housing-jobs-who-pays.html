Housing, jobs and pay: who pays, and do the jobs cover the cost of living here?

The state and Ogden have offered tax credits and tax increment to bring jobs to Weber County. Here are the jobs and pay the companies promised, what the incentives are worth, what it costs to rent or buy in Ogden, and which local budgets share in the cost.

Area: Ogden and Weber CountyRecords: Go Utah incented companies, Ogden RDA annual report, CensusYears: 2004–2026

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHO AND WHAT
Ogden · Weber County · the State of Utah
  • The jobs deals: Williams International, Northrop Grumman, Fresenius and Amer Sports signed state tax credit contracts for jobs in the Ogden area.
  • The local piece: Ogden's Redevelopment Agency (RDA) uses tax increment from its project areas, including up to $119.3 million from the Airport project area over 25 years.
  • The cost of living: It takes about $66,196 a year to rent and $125,216 to buy the typical Ogden home (Redfin, Dec. 2025).
  • Who shares in the cost: Ogden School District, Ogden City, Weber County and Central Weber Sewer each agreed to send part of their tax growth to the Airport project area.

In plain words (explain it like I'm 10)

A company says, "We will hire people in Ogden." The state says, "If you really do, we will give you back some of the new state taxes you pay." That is a tax credit. The company only gets it after the jobs show up. Ogden can also help. When land in a special area gets more valuable, its owners pay more property tax. Everyone still pays the full tax, and the county treasurer collects it. But for a set number of years, the extra part goes to the special area instead of to the school district, city and county budgets. That extra part is called tax increment. The big question on this page: do the jobs pay enough to live here? To rent the typical Ogden place, a household needs about $66,000 a year. To buy the typical Ogden home, it needs about $125,000. Most of the promised jobs pay enough to rent. None of the promised pay we found reaches what it takes to buy.

The short version

  • Williams International (2024): 316 promised jobs, about $82,600 pay per job a year (Hive arithmetic from state projections). State credit up to $29.0 million, plus $22 million in Ogden tax increment.
  • Northrop Grumman: 2,250 promised jobs in Weber County (2020) at about $99,700 a year, and 1,206 more across northern Utah (2024) at about $114,200 (Hive arithmetic).
  • Weber County's average wage is $58,108 (state table, 2025). To buy the typical Ogden home takes about $125,216 a year.
  • Jobs actually created are not published for any of these deals. The state shows only how much of each credit has been paid, in 25% ranges.
  • Ogden's RDA reported $69.6 million received so far across its older project areas (Hive sum of the RDA's own table). The Airport area began collecting in 2025.

Ogden's typical household earns just over what it takes to rent, and about $55,000 short of what it takes to buy (Hive arithmetic; the figures are from different years).

Income-restricted homes

  • Ogden has 2,503 income-restricted (tax credit) units operating and 1,275 more on the way (Utah Housing Corp., via KUER, Jan. 2, 2026).
  • A one-bedroom at 60% of area income in one developer's buildings rents for $1,356 a month, about 32% of income for a renter at the top of the eligible range (KUER).
  • Weber County's latest breakdown by income (2018) found the shortage at the lowest incomes (0–30% of area income), with surpluses at 30–80%.

Where the process followed the rules

  • State credits are paid after the fact. EDTIF credits are paid only after a company reports the jobs and new state taxes. A company that leaves early doesn't get the unearned part.
  • Public board. Awards are approved by the Go Utah board in public meetings and posted in the Incented Companies table.
  • Contest period. The Airport project area's agreements passed the 30-day contest period with no protests (KSL).
  • Written terms. Agreements like Weber County's Flagship agreement spell out the share, the years, the cap and the housing set-aside.

What we don't know yet

  • How many people each company actually employs under its contract, and what it actually pays. The state publishes only projections and the share of each credit paid, in 25% ranges.
  • Whether Ogden's $22 million Airport-area incentive to Williams International depends on the 316 jobs, and whether it can be taken back.
  • How much each taxing entity has actually contributed to each Ogden project area, by year.
  • How much each project area has set aside for housing, and what that money bought.
  • Whether the 2024 Northrop Grumman jobs are in Ogden or Weber County, or elsewhere in northern Utah.
  • Average pay per job for each award. The table shows total wages, not an average.

Each of these is turned into a question below.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  1. Go Utah board (public meetings) or the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity: For Williams International, Northrop Grumman, Fresenius and Amer Sports: how many jobs has each company reported under its contract, at what average pay?
  2. Ogden City Council, acting as the RDA board (public comment): Is the $22 million Airport-area incentive to Williams International tied to the 316 jobs, and is there a clawback if they don't show up?
  3. Ogden RDA (City Council at public comment): How much has each taxing entity contributed to each project area by year, and how much of each area's money has gone to the required housing set-aside?
  4. Ogden School District board: The district's share of the Airport area is about $68.55 million over 25 years. What does the district get back, and how did the board decide?
  5. Weber County Commission: How much has the county contributed to Ogden's project areas by year, and from which funds?
  6. Go Utah: Where are the 1,206 jobs in Northrop Grumman's 2024 award located, and what is the promised average pay?
  7. Ogden City Council and Planning Commission: The Plan Ogden draft removes "protect existing naturally affordable housing" downtown. What protects people living in those homes now?
