CASE FILE · OGDEN CITY · LOBBYING AND OUTSIDE MONEY · OC 009

Who lobbies for Ogden, what it pays, and what money came back

Ogden City pays two lobbying firms: one at the Utah Capitol and one in Washington, D.C. Here is what the city paid each year, what the lobbyists report, and the state and federal money Ogden has received.

Payer: Ogden CityYears: 2014–2026Records: Transparent Utah, state and federal lobbyist filings

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHO AND WHAT
Ogden City · RRJ Consulting (state) · The Bennett Group (federal)
  • State lobbying: RRJ Consulting's lobbyists have listed Ogden City as a client since 2012–2013. Legislative Solutions / NRAE Solutions lobbyists, including Kerry Gibson, list Ogden from 2024.
  • Federal lobbying: The Bennett Consulting Group (Washington, D.C., contact Kenneth Lee) reports the City of Ogden as a federal lobbying client.
  • What Ogden paid: RRJ: $210,000 in fiscal 2025. Bennett Group DC: $765,000 in fiscal 2026 (Transparent Utah).
  • Money to Ogden: A $42 million federal WIFIA water loan announced Aug. 12, 2024, and a 2024 state request of $10 million for the Ogden Canyon water line.

In plain words

Cities hire lobbyists to ask the Legislature and Congress for money and to watch bills that affect them. That is legal and common. Ogden pays one firm, RRJ Consulting, for state work, and RRJ pays lobbyist Kerry Gibson for his part. Ogden pays another firm, the Bennett Group, for work in Washington. Both bills to the city went up sharply after 2023. Over the same years, Ogden asked for and received state and federal money for its water line. The records show the payments and the money. They do not show, by themselves, which lobbying produced which dollar.

The short version

  • RRJ Consulting (state): Ogden City paid $60,000 in fiscal 2023, then $105,000 (2024), $210,000 (2025) and $155,000 (2026).
  • Bennett Group DC (federal): Ogden City paid $55,200 in fiscal 2023, then $180,582.80 (2024), $427,000 (2025) and $765,000 (2026).
  • Bennett's own federal lobbying reports list $13,800 a quarter ($55,200 a year) in income from Ogden for 2025, on "Transportation Issues."
  • $42 million: EPA announced a WIFIA loan to Ogden City on Aug. 12, 2024, for its water line replacement.
  • The Hive's own records request for Ogden's records of its work with Kerry Gibson came back "no records found"; the city pays RRJ, and RRJ pays Gibson.

What the federal lobbying reports say

Federal lobbyists file quarterly reports with Congress. The Bennett Consulting Group (122 C Street NW, Washington, D.C.; contact Kenneth Lee) lists the City of Ogden, Utah as a client. Its four 2025 reports each list $13,800 in income, lobbying the House and Senate on "Transportation Issues."

Ogden's payments to Bennett Group DC in the same period were much higher: $427,000 in fiscal 2025 and $765,000 in fiscal 2026. The federal reports and the city's payment records don't explain the difference. Federal reports may count only lobbying income, and the firm's federal registration name ("The Bennett Consulting Group") differs from the city's vendor name ("Bennett Group DC"). That's a question for the city, not a finding.

State and federal money to Ogden

Federal

  • $42 million WIFIA loan: the EPA announced the loan to Ogden City on Aug. 12, 2024, for the replacement of its main feed line and the transmission line from the water treatment plant to Ogden Canyon. EPA's project page lists $42 million as the "Invited WIFIA Loan Amount." The announcement also listed $34 million from Utah's Drinking Water State Revolving Fund. It's a loan, repaid with interest, not a grant.

Success fees in Ogden's records

Being re-read before we rely on itOgden's response to an earlier Hive records request (G015679-070626) lists the Bennett Group's terms as a $4,600-a-month retainer plus federal lobbying success fees: $420,000 tied to the "WIFIA loan" and $110,000 tied to "trails funding." These terms come from the Hive's earlier review of that response; we are re-reading the documents and will update this section with exact wording and dates.

Arithmetic only: $420,000 is 1% of $42 million. The records we've read don't say how the fee was set, when it was paid, or which payments in the table above it's part of. Success fees are a known practice in government relations; the question for Ogden is what the contract says and whether the council saw it.

The same records response shows Kerry Gibson works for Ogden as a subcontractor to RRJ Consulting. RRJ's invoices list his work at $30,000 (February 2024) and $40,000 (April 2025), according to the Hive's earlier review of those invoices.

Our records request about Kerry Gibson

In June 2026, the Hive asked Ogden City for its records of its engagement with Kerry Gibson (request G015529-061226). The city answered "no records found," even though Gibson's state registration lists Ogden City as a client from Jan. 19, 2024. A later response (G015679-070626) explained the arrangement: the city's contract is with RRJ Consulting, and RRJ pays Gibson. So the city's own files show RRJ, not Gibson.

What we don't know yet

  • What RRJ Consulting's work for Ogden covers, and why payments rose from $60,000 (FY2023) to $210,000 (FY2025).
  • Why Ogden's payments to Bennett Group DC ($427,000 in FY2025; $765,000 in FY2026) are far above the income Bennett reported federally ($55,200 in 2025).
  • The exact wording, dates and payment status of the success fees in Ogden's records response, now being re-read.
  • What federal program the $110,000 trails fee relates to, and how much money it brought.
  • Whether the 2026 Union Station request was funded.
  • Which State of Utah payments to Ogden were grants and which were reimbursements or pass-through money.
  • Whether the City Council approved each lobbying contract and its fee terms in a public meeting.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  1. Ogden City Council: Did the council approve the Bennett Group and RRJ contracts and their success-fee terms? When?
  2. Ogden City administration: What do the FY2025 and FY2026 Bennett payments cover, and how do they match the firm's federal reports?
  3. Ogden City administration: What did RRJ Consulting and its subcontractors work on in 2024–2026?
  4. Legislators who sponsored Ogden requests: Which Ogden requests were funded, and who asked for them?
