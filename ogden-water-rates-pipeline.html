Ogden's water rates and the $99 million pipeline

Not every rate increase is the same. Ogden tied its 2024 water rate proposal to replacing a leaking, 90-year-old water line, and laid out how it would be paid for.

Project: Ogden Canyon water lineCost: $99 million (KSL, June 2024)Leak: about 3 million gallons a day

Posted Nov. 6, 2026 · Updated Nov. 6, 2026

WHAT
Ogden Canyon water line replacement
  • The line: 6.4 miles, 36 inches wide, about 90 years old; the project replaces 7.25 miles in three segments (KSL, June 7, 2024).
  • Why: The city said the line leaks about 3 million gallons a day.
  • Funding plan: $10 million from the state, $12 million in federal ARPA funds, about $77 million to $82.1 million in state and federal loans repaid over 40 years (KSL).

In plain words

Ogden's main water line runs through Ogden Canyon and is about 90 years old. The city said it leaks about 3 million gallons a day. In June 2024 the city proposed raising water rates 4% a year for five years, plus inflation adjustments, to help pay the loans for a $99 million replacement. This page shows what the city said the money is for, so readers can compare it with other increases.

Explain it like I'm 10

  • Ogden's main water pipe runs through Ogden Canyon. It is about 90 years old.
  • The city says it leaks about 3 million gallons of water a day.
  • A new pipe costs about $99 million.
  • To help pay the loans, the city planned to raise water rates 4% a year for five years.
  • This page shows what the city said the money is for.

The short version

  • Cost: $99 million to replace 7.25 miles of the Ogden Canyon line (KSL, June 7, 2024).
  • Proposed rates: base and tiered rates up 4% a year for five years, plus inflation adjustments. KSL reported a typical household (5,000 gallons a month) would go from $442.68 to $555.48 a year by fiscal 2029, an increase of 25.5% in KSL's example (4% a year for five years compounds to about 21.7%; KSL's figure includes other adjustments).
  • Revenue: an estimated $1.6 million to $2.2 million a year toward the loan payments.
  • Grants and loans: $10 million state, $12 million federal ARPA, about $77 million to $82.1 million in loans over 40 years (KSL).
  • Property tax: the Standard-Examiner reported Ogden adopted a $289.8 million FY2026 budget with no tax increases (June 19, 2025).

The pipeline

KSL reported (June 7, 2024) that the main Ogden Canyon line is 6.4 miles long, 36 inches wide and about 90 years old, and that the project replaces 7.25 miles of pipe in three segments. The city said the line loses about 3 million gallons a day. The work is why Pineview Reservoir was lowered in 2026 (Deseret News and KSL, June 26, 2026).

How this compares

The Hive's standard is to show when a process discloses what the money is for, not just when it doesn't. Here the city named the project, the cost, the leak, the grants, the loans and the yearly rate path before the public hearing. That's different from a tax increment project area, where, for an agreed term, the agreed share of the growth in property taxes is allocated to the project area instead of the taxing entities' budgets.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether the council adopted the rates exactly as proposed in June 2024, and the final vote. (The June 18, 2024 meeting results weren't read for this page.)
  • Whether the project is on budget and on schedule as of November 2026.
  • How much the leak has dropped as segments are finished.

Every item above is also a question you can ask. See Questions to ask, and whom to ask.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  1. Ogden Public Services: What has the pipeline cost so far, and is it on budget?
  2. City Council: Will the remaining rate steps change if costs come in lower or higher?
  3. Ogden City Recorder: Do the June 18, 2024 meeting results show the water rates adopted and each member's vote?
  4. Ogden Public Services: When is the full line expected to be finished?
  5. Ogden Public Services: How much has the leak dropped as sections of the new line are finished?
