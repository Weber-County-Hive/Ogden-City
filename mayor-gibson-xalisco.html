CASE FILE · OGDEN MAYOR · LOBBYING AND CONTRACTS · OC 005

The mayor, Kerry Gibson and Xalisco

A political action committee, a campaign vendor, a city lobbyist and a change to Ogden's contracting rules. Here is what each record shows, and what it doesn't.

Office: Ogden mayorYears: 2023–2026Records: state, city, Transparent Utah

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHO AND WHAT
Mayor Ben Nadolski · lobbyist Kerry Gibson · Xalisco, LLC · RRJ Consulting
  • 2023 campaign: Utah Forward PAC reported giving $5,000 to the Committee to Elect Ben Nadolski.
  • City lobbyist: Kerry Gibson's state lobbyist registration lists Ogden City as a client from Jan. 19, 2024.
  • How he's paid: Ogden pays RRJ Consulting, and RRJ pays Gibson, according to Ogden's records response.
  • Contracting rules: Ordinance 2025-2, adopted 6–0 on Jan. 21, 2025, changed how the city hires professional services.

In plain words

In 2023, a political action committee gave money to Ben Nadolski's campaign for mayor, and his campaign paid a catering company called Xalisco, which lobbyist Kerry Gibson is a member of. After Nadolski took office, Gibson registered as a lobbyist for Ogden City. The city pays a consulting firm, RRJ, and RRJ pays Gibson. The city also paid Xalisco for catering, including the mayor's State of the City event. In 2025 the council changed the rules so the city can hire professional services up to $100,000 without formal bidding. None of these records, by themselves, show that one thing caused another. They show the connections, and they raise questions only the officials can answer.

The short version

  • $5,000: Utah Forward PAC to the Committee to Elect Ben Nadolski, dated March 8, 2023 in the PAC's state report.
  • $3,187.50: Nadolski's 2023 campaign paid Xalisco, LLC (members include Kerry Gibson and Luis Octavio Lopez).
  • Jan. 19, 2024: Kerry Gibson's lobbyist registration lists Ogden City as a client.
  • RRJ Consulting: Ogden City paid RRJ $105,000 in fiscal 2024, $210,000 in fiscal 2025 and $155,000 in fiscal 2026, up from $60,000 in fiscal 2023 (Transparent Utah). RRJ's invoices list Gibson's work at $30,000 (Feb. 2024) and $40,000 (April 2025).
  • Ordinance 2025-2 (adopted 6–0, Jan. 21, 2025) lets the city hire professional services up to $100,000 per project by direct negotiation after reviewing at least three firms.

The lobbyist

Kerry Gibson's lobbyist registration with the state lists Ogden City as a client from Jan. 19, 2024. Ogden told the Hive it made no payments to Gibson directly; the city pays RRJ Consulting, and RRJ pays him, so city records don't show what his work covers.

Gibson is also tied to other Hive case files: Plain City hired Lincoln Hill Partners, where he is a partner, and paid his Legislative Solutions $42,000 for 2025 (see OC 001).

Xalisco

Xalisco, LLC's members, per the Utah Division of Corporations, include Kerry Gibson, Luis Octavio Lopez and David Lopez-Carrillo. Ogden City's payment records, provided to the Hive, show about 37 payments to Xalisco totaling about $14,000 from July 2023 to May 2026, including $9,800 for the mayor's State of the City event (Feb. 2025).

Transparent Utah's vendor search does not list Ogden City payments under "Xalisco" (it lists two small Ogden School District payments), so the city figures here come from the city's own records response, not the state site.

Ordinance 2025-2: the contracting change

On Jan. 7, 2025, city staff presented "Proposed Purchasing Code Amendments" to the council. On Jan. 21, 2025, the council adopted Ordinance 2025-2, amending the city code chapters on contracts (4-2A) and professional services (4-2B), 6–0.

The staff packet describes the new rule for professional services: contracts under $100,000 per project by "direct negotiation after reviewing qualifications of three (3) individuals/firms," and formal solicitation over $100,000. Its comparison table lists Ogden's previous formal-bid level for professional services at $10,000, against an average of $79,000 for the other cities it surveyed.

Correction note: an earlier Hive article named this "Ordinance 2025-1" in one place. Ordinance 2025-1 (Jan. 7, 2025) was a campaign finance ordinance. The procurement change is Ordinance 2025-2. The comparison table was read from the PDF's text layer; check the rendered PDF before quoting individual cells.

Luis Octavio Lopez

Lopez, a former two-term Ogden City Council member and a Xalisco member, returned to city government as Community Engagement Director in about July 2025. The Standard-Examiner reported (Jan. 21, 2026) that Mayor Nadolski asked him to apply.

The mayor's response

The Hive asked Mayor Nadolski five questions in June 2026. His June 15, 2026 email response began: "The relationship you are asking about has always been discussed openly and publicly." His written response said Xalisco received "no city contracts"; city payment records later showed the payments listed above. The Hive's question letter referred to "Ordinance 2025-1"; his answer addressed that ordinance, not the procurement change in Ordinance 2025-2.

What we don't know yet

  • What RRJ's payments from Ogden cover in total, and how much of each year's total went to Gibson.
  • Whether Gibson lobbied on Ordinance 2025-2. His scope of work for Ogden isn't public.
  • Whether any contract has been awarded to a Gibson-connected business under the new $100,000 direct-negotiation rule.
  • The individual votes on Ordinance 2025-2 (the meeting results list only 6–0).

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  1. Mayor Nadolski: What does RRJ Consulting do for Ogden, and what share of its payments goes to Kerry Gibson?
  2. City Council: Which professional services contracts since Jan. 2025 were awarded by direct negotiation, and to whom?
  3. City Recorder: Will the city post a list of direct-negotiation contracts so residents don't have to request it?
