Before Tuesday: Ogden's police immigration policy

Mayor Ben Nadolski and Police Chief Jake Sube are scheduled to update the City Council on Nov. 10 on a written policy for how Ogden police handle federal immigration enforcement. The council does not approve police policy. Here is what is on the record so far.

Department: Ogden PoliceWork session: Tue., Nov. 10, 2026, 4 p.m.Council action: none (update only)

Posted Nov. 6, 2026 · Updated Nov. 6, 2026

WHAT'S ON THE AGENDA
Administrative Update – Ogden Police Department Immigration Policy and Procedure
  • When: City Council and RDA joint work session, Tue., Nov. 10, 2026, 4 p.m., Council Work Room, 2549 Washington Blvd., and Zoom.
  • Who presents: Mayor Ben Nadolski and Police Chief Jake Sube requested the time, according to the council staff review.
  • What the council can do: Listen, ask questions and discuss. The staff review says: "The Council does not approve police department or other administrative policies."

In plain words

A federal immigration officer arrested a man in Ogden on Aug. 25, 2026, while an Ogden police officer was on the scene for a different call. Since then, residents and some council members have asked the city to write down how Ogden officers should act when that happens. Utah law says cities can't forbid their officers from cooperating with immigration enforcement, but it doesn't require officers to help, either. The police chief is writing the policy. On Tuesday he is expected to explain where it stands. Weber County's sheriff, a separate government, has a formal agreement with ICE; Ogden's police department does not.

Explain it like I'm 10

  • ICE is the federal agency that enforces immigration laws.
  • On Aug. 25, 2026, an ICE officer arrested a man in Ogden while an Ogden police officer was there.
  • Utah law says a city can't tell its police they are not allowed to help ICE.
  • But the law doesn't make Ogden police help, either.
  • People asked the city to write down how its officers should act. The police chief is working on that.
  • On Tuesday, Nov. 10, the council gets an update.

The short version

  • The Nov. 10 item is an update, not a vote. The staff review says Chief Sube "is expected to provide an update on where the department is in the process of developing and finalizing a policy."
  • It follows the Aug. 25, 2026 arrest by an ICE agent while an Ogden officer was present on an unrelated call. Ogden police said the department "did not initiate the stop, did not conduct the arrest, did not ask for or run any information, and did not assist with immigration enforcement."
  • Utah Code §76-14-205 bars local governments from limiting cooperation with federal immigration enforcement. It does not create a duty for officers to help.
  • Weber County's sheriff has a 287(g) agreement with ICE. Ogden's police department does not, and city leaders have said publicly it won't pursue one.
  • The Hive's full case file on the Aug. 25 incident is in the Unelected Power repo (link below).

The agenda item

The council staff review for Nov. 10 says Mayor Nadolski and Chief Sube "have requested time on the City Council's work session agenda to provide an update on the administration's work on a policy related to Ogden Police Department's immigration enforcement procedures." Chief Sube is expected to describe "where the department is in the process of developing and finalizing a policy" and the department's approach. The council can listen, ask questions and discuss.

The same staff review notes that updates to the department's Policy 64 on automated license plate readers were presented to the council on Oct. 13, 2026. Current police policies are listed on the department's webpage.

Who decides: under the staff review, the policy is the administration's. The council hears it but does not adopt it.

What happened Aug. 25, 2026

An Ogden police officer was on an unrelated call when a federal immigration agent arrested a man identified on the officer's body camera only as "Jonathan." Ogden police released the body camera video the next day. The department's statement said it "did not initiate the stop, did not conduct the arrest, did not ask for or run any information, and did not assist with immigration enforcement. The officer's involvement was limited to maintaining safety and attempting to de-escalate an unusual and potentially escalating interaction."

Some residents and council members read the video differently. At the Sept. 1, 2026 council meeting, Councilwoman Alicia Washington called for a full review, and Councilman Ken Richey defended the officer. The Hive's case file walks through the video, the statements and the questions in detail.

What state law says

Utah Code §76-14-205 says local governments may not limit or prohibit their officers from cooperating with federal officials on immigration enforcement. It does not require local officers to take part. In 2026, H.B. 571, a bill that would have required local police to use "best efforts" to cooperate, did not pass; it never left the House Rules Committee and was filed as not passed on March 6, 2026 (Better Utah bill tracker).

In March 2026, Council members Kevin Lundell and Alicia Washington withdrew a draft council resolution, "Affirming Community Trust Through Public Safety." KSL reported on Feb. 18, 2026 that they paused it because it might conflict with H.B. 571, then a pending bill, and planned to take it up again March 3. The March withdrawal and its stated reason are per the Hive's earlier case file, not re-checked. That leaves the policy with the police chief.

What we don't know yet

  • What the draft policy says. No draft is posted with the Nov. 10 agenda.
  • Whether the policy will be posted publicly once it is final, and when.
  • Whether the officer's actions on Aug. 25 were reviewed and what any review found. The chief has said he doesn't believe policy was violated (per news coverage cited in the Hive case file).
  • How the federal agent located the man before the arrest. Nothing published says.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  1. City Council, at the Nov. 10 work session or by email to the council: Will the final policy be posted, and will residents see a draft before it is final?
  2. City Council public comment (about the police department's draft policy): What should an officer do when a federal agent asks for help on a call that has nothing to do with immigration?
  3. City Council public comment (about the administration's draft policy): Will the policy say that people can call Ogden police for help without immigration questions being asked?
  4. City Council (citycouncil@ogdencity.com): Will the council hold a follow-up with public comment once the policy is final?
  5. Ogden City Council (Nov. 10 work session or public comment): What does the draft policy say, and will it be posted before the council discusses it again?
  6. Ogden City Council (public comment): Was the Aug. 25 call reviewed under department policy, and will the result be shared with the council?
  7. Ogden City Council (public comment): Did any Ogden police record or system help federal agents locate the man before the Aug. 25 arrest, and will the new policy address information sharing?
